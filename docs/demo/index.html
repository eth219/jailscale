What the first byte of this page cost

One visitor arriving, from the browser's own navigation timing. TLS is the part that is mostly jailscale: the hub routes on the SNI alone, opens a stream to the node, signs one handshake digest bound to that stream, and the node — not the hub — finishes the session. TCP is your round trip to the hub and nothing else: the floor under everything above it.

Round trip, request by request

Sequential GET /ping on one open connection, so no handshake and no signature — just the relay copying bytes, twice across your network. The app's own time is stamped in a Server-Timing header and subtracted, so what is plotted is the path, not the app.

What this measures, and what it does not

  • The network floor is not jailscale's to beat. The TCP connect time above is your round trip to the hub. Every other number on this page contains it at least once.
  • The handshake is the expensive path, and it is the interesting one. It is the only part where the hub does cryptographic work per visitor, and the hub signs at most 1,000 handshakes a second for any one node, 2,000 in a burst.
  • A warm round trip is the relay, and the relay is cheap. On loopback, with the network removed, the pair moves about 38,000 requests a second on connections already open. What you see here is mostly distance.
  • The hub in the path may be very small. The reference hub runs on a GCP e2-micro — 2 shared vCPU, 1 GB — and that is not the constraint.
  • None of this is a budget number. measure.sh runs both native binaries on loopback and gates binary size, idle RSS, peak RSS under 1,000 held visitors and CLI cold start. A page measured over the internet can only tell you what a visitor felt.
